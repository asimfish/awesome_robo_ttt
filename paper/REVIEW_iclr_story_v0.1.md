# Review: "What Does a Derivative Action Space Buy a Diffusion Policy? A Controlled Map of First-Order Action Filters"

**Reviewer role:** ICLR area chair / reviewer (robot learning, RL fine-tuning of imitation policies, control-theoretic action spaces)
**Scope of this review:** story, framing, novelty, positioning, logical soundness of claims, writing/clarity. Experimental scale (one task, one training seed for most points, one simulator) is deliberately **not** discussed; every judgement below is conditional on the experiments being adequate.
**Files reviewed:** `paper/main.tex` (line references below are to this file), `paper/main.pdf`, `proposal/CADI_TTT_PROPOSAL_v2.md` Sec. 1.5-1.10 and Sec. 6, `figures/DS_map.png`, `figures/DS_h9.png`, `figures/P1_upstream.png`.

Notation in this review: \(\sigma_{\rm exec} = s\sigma/\sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\) and \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) are the paper's executed and chunk-scale exploration noise; "obs" means the filter state is observed by the policy.

---

## 1. The story as I understood it

The authors set out to propose a "derivative action interface" for RL fine-tuning of a diffusion policy (the policy outputs an increment, a leaky integrator accumulates it, and the integrator state is appended to the observation) and found, against strong controls, that almost none of the intended benefits survived. They then recast the object of study: every velocity/derivative/low-pass interface is one first-order filter \(c_t=\lambda c_{t-1}+s u_t\), so "raw", "low-pass", and "derivative" are points in a \((\lambda,s)\) plane, plus two extra coordinates: the training exploration noise \(\sigma\) and whether the policy sees the filter state. Twelve trained configurations are evaluated on five axes. The three findings that carry the paper are: (i) RL trainability and zero-shot drift robustness are functions of the *executed* exploration noise \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) (better: its chunk-scale, colour-aware variant), not of filter structure, so the derivative interface's apparent advantages over its matched-\(\sigma\) twin were an artefact of comparing two under-explored policies; (ii) the behaviour-cloning prior improves as \(s\) shrinks because the relabelled targets then fill the diffusion model's output range; (iii) exposing the filter state to the policy, which is necessary for the derivative labels to be identifiable, makes the policy output the pre-whitened demonstration sequence, so the executed trajectory recovers the demonstration's own spectrum (no smoothing of intent), upstream adaptive corrections are fought by the policy, and residual signals become harder to read. A five-control protocol, each control paired with the false conclusion it prevented, is offered as a methodological contribution.

The story is legible, but I had to do the synthesis myself. The single fact that organizes everything -- **with the filter state observed, the relabelling is an exact change of coordinates (\(c_t=a_t\) whenever clipping is inactive), so the interface cannot change the intended trajectory, only the channel through which perturbations reach the plant** -- is implied by eqs. (1)-(2) (L39, L70) and by the "innovation" remark (L173), but is never stated. Once stated, findings (ii) and (iii) become corollaries and finding (i) becomes the statement that the perturbation channel is the *only* thing the interface changes. The current draft instead presents the results as five axes on a map and asks the reader to infer the law.

## 2. Verdict on the story (ICLR main track)

**Score for story/contribution quality, assuming adequate experiments: 5/10 as written (marginally below the acceptance threshold). With the reframing proposed in Sec. 5 and the fixes to W1-W5 below, I would expect 6-7.**

Justification. The paper contains one genuinely general and slightly surprising structural result (observable-state action filters are exact reparameterizations; therefore they shape exploration and adaptation noise, never intent, and a trainable derivative prior and a smoothing filter are mutually exclusive), one useful methodological result (comparisons between action spaces must match executed, chunk-scale noise, not the policy-side noise parameter; most of the paper's own early conclusions failed this test), and a decomposition of "what a derivative action space buys" into ingredients that each have their own literature (previous-action conditioning, target normalization, coloured exploration noise). That is enough material for an ICLR analysis paper of the "Implementation Matters" / "Pink Noise" type, whose bar is a crisp claim that changes what practitioners do. But the draft does not yet make that claim. It is framed as a project post-mortem ("we started from exactly such a proposal", L35; "ten days and roughly nine hundred evaluation rollouts", L35), its headline statistic in the abstract is misreported (\(\rho = 0.92\) is for nine points excluding the authors' own interface, not "across ten points", L28 vs L202), its Contribution 3(a) contradicts Sec. VI-D on whether the original interface's fragility is explained, and its most "counter-intuitive" finding (prior improves as \(s\) shrinks) is target normalization. A skeptical ICLR reviewer will write "more exploration noise gives more robustness is known; the whitening argument is an identity; the protocol is common sense validated on one project." A sympathetic one will write "honest, careful, and I learned something about action-space comparisons." The average of those two is a 5. The material to get to a 7 is already in the paper; what is missing is the thesis sentence, two cheap controls that close the factorization (Sec. 4, W3), and a related-work section that engages the literature the reframed paper actually belongs to.

Venue: the draft as it stands is a natural RA-L/ICRA paper (IEEEtran format, "what does X buy", a recommended configuration). See Sec. 5 for what would differ.

## 3. Strengths

- **S1. The unification is right and useful (L37-42, L68-73).** Writing raw, low-pass, and derivative interfaces as \((\lambda,s)\) points with a binary observability flag is the correct abstraction, and the paper draws the correct conclusion that DC gain is irrelevant to prior alignment (F5 vs F1/F3, L154). Few action-space papers place their proposal on a common axis with the null hypothesis.
- **S2. The observability dichotomy is the real discovery (L73, L171-173, L215).** The pair B/F4 (unobservable) vs F7/F5 (observable) at identical \((\lambda,s,\sigma)\) is the cleanest experiment in the paper. The trio "whitens the output / blocks upstream adaptation / degrades residual identifiability" is one phenomenon seen three times, and the paper (almost) says so.
- **S3. The matched-twin vs strongest-baseline distinction (L44, L101, L234) is a real methodological point** and is stated crisply in the Discussion: the twin answers the *mechanism* question, the strongest baseline answers the *value* question, and the interface's noise gain is what confounds them.
- **S4. The chunk-scale, colour-aware noise (L82, L202) is a non-obvious refinement.** It explains why the raw policy at \(\sigma = 0.03\) is as fragile as the low-pass at \(\sigma_{\rm exec} = 0.010\): what the policy can learn to correct is the displacement visible within one decision period. This is a mechanism statement, not a correlation, and it is testable (vary \(H\)).
- **S5. The pre-registration discipline is visible and creditable.** The proposal documents predictions written before results (H9 intervals, F5/F6 as discriminating points for H10), and the paper reports the rejected hypotheses (integral compensation, L103; prior quality as second factor, L204). This is rare and should be preserved in the appendix.
- **S6. Honest handling of the residual (L204, L238).** The E4/F7 anomaly is reported as unexplained rather than explained away.
- **S7. The prose is generally precise** and the Discussion (L234-236) contains two of the best sentences in the paper: "the twin is the correct control for mechanism but the wrong control for value", and "any claim of smoother execution should be checked on deterministic rollouts against an unobservable low-pass filter of the same bound."

## 4. Weaknesses in the story, ranked by severity

### W1 (critical). The thesis is present but never stated; the paper is organized around a question ("what does it buy") instead of an answer ("what it is").

**What is wrong.** Substituting eq. (2) into eq. (1): \(c_t=\lambda c_{t-1}+(a_t-\lambda c_{t-1})=a_t\) whenever clipping is inactive (under 1% of steps, L154). The observable-state derivative interface is therefore *a position policy conditioned on its previous executed command, with its output target affinely rescaled to \((a_t-\lambda a_{t-1})/s\)*. Nothing about integration survives in the intended trajectory. What the filter does act on is anything added in \(u\)-coordinates: exploration noise, model error, adaptation corrections. Every finding in the paper follows: the prior effect is conditioning plus target scaling (VI-A); the deterministic executed spectrum is the demonstration's (VI-C); the smoothing of exploration noise is real (Prop. 1, VI-C last sentence); upstream corrections are treated by the policy as disturbances to \(c\) and undone (VI-E); and the unobservable low-pass is the *only* way to smooth intent, at the price of a prior mismatch that cannot be trained away (the 0%-success pilot, L73). The paper says "differing from a plain low-pass filter only in its DC gain and in whether the filter state is exposed" (L28), and then shows DC gain does not matter -- so the whole difference is observability, and observability means "the policy inverts the filter."

**Why a reviewer pushes.** Without the identity, the five axes look like five separate empirical facts about Square, and the paper reads as "we ran a grid." With it, four of the five axes are predicted before any rollout, and the map becomes verification of a claim that holds for any policy class and any task. This is the difference between an anecdote and a result.

**Fix.** State the identity as the first proposition in Sec. III, immediately after L73, and derive the corollaries: (a) executed trajectory = intended trajectory + filtered model error (so observable-state points are smoother than raw by exactly the model-error filtering -- the 20-55% jerk reduction in Table II -- and not by the intent filtering that gives the unobservable low-pass 80%); (b) the policy output is the demonstration pre-whitened by \(\lambda\) (see W5 for the exact prediction); (c) upstream scaling of \(u\) is a disturbance the policy is trained to reject; (d) impossibility: a filter either is observed (then it cannot smooth intent) or is not (then the derivative labels are not identifiable and the position prior is mismatched). Then organize Results by *channel* (intent vs perturbation), not by axis. The title and abstract should carry the identity, not the question.

### W2 (critical). Three claim/evidence mismatches that damage credibility.

1. **L28 abstract: "Spearman \(\rho=0.92\) across ten points."** L202 says \(\rho = 0.76\) across the ten stationary points for both metrics, rising to 0.92 for the chunk-scale metric *when E4 is excluded*. The excluded point is the paper's own original interface and its principal outlier. Reporting the nine-point number as a ten-point number in the abstract is the kind of error that costs a paper its reviewers' trust in every other number.
2. **L50 Contribution 3(a): "high-DC-gain interfaces amplify noise and therefore force training at low \(\sigma\), which is why the original interface was fragile."** Sec. VI-D (L204) says E4 is the point the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) law does *not* explain: at \(\sigma_{\rm exec} = 0.069\) the trend predicts roughly 50-60% and E4 gets 28%. The noise-gain argument explains at most the gap from A's 81% down to the trend value; the rest is the unexplained observability penalty. The contribution statement and the results section disagree about the paper's own central example.
3. **L28 abstract: "whitens the policy output so that the executed trajectory is no smoother than the demonstrations."** The evidence (Table II) is that E4's spectral exponent (1.83) matches the raw policy's (1.76); the other observable-state points have \(\beta_c = 1.98\)-2.25 and jerk 0.07-0.13 against raw 0.16-0.18, i.e., they *are* smoother than raw, just by model-error filtering rather than intent filtering (W1). The demonstration spectrum itself is never measured. "No smoother than demonstrations" is asserted, not shown.

**Fix.** Report 0.76 (ten) and 0.92 (nine, E4 excluded) side by side everywhere; rewrite 3(a) to separate the explained and unexplained parts; replace "no smoother than the demonstrations" with the exact statement from W1(a) and measure the demonstration command spectrum directly from the dataset (zero rollouts needed).

### W3 (major). The prior-alignment finding is target normalization plus previous-action conditioning, sold as counter-intuitive, and the factorization has two holes.

**What is wrong.** L154: "with s = 1 the closed-loop labels have mean magnitude |u| of about 0.09 and are dominated by the diffusion model's output noise; with s = 0.3 they fill the [-1, 1] range." That *is* the standard "normalize your regression targets" rule, made specific to a generative model with a fixed output-noise floor in normalized units. The "common intuition" it contradicts (L28, L50) is the authors' own pre-registered guess (proposal Sec. 1.10, Q1), not a community belief; an ICLR reviewer's intuition is the opposite. Separately, the 38 to 59.5 gain (A to F5) confounds three things: (i) conditioning on the previous command, (ii) target rescaling by \(1/s\), (iii) training on relabelled data at all. The map has no point that isolates (i) from (ii): the \((\lambda,s)=(0,1)\) point *with observable state* is literally "raw policy + previous action in the observation", and \((0,s<1)\) with observable state is "raw policy + rescaled target." Both are one BC run each.

**Why a reviewer pushes.** "Your headline surprise is a normalization effect" is an easy review to write. And the paper's decomposition of the derivative interface into ingredients is incomplete without the two corner points -- the reviewer cannot tell whether the prior effect is conditioning, scaling, or both. This is a logical gap in the argument, not a scale complaint.

**Fix.** Reframe 3(b) as: "\(s\) is a target-normalization knob; the derivative labels at \(s=1\) sit below the diffusion model's output-noise floor." Say plainly that this is the known rule, applied to a place people forget it (relabelled derivative targets), and make the diffusion-specific point (fixed noise floor means \(s\) controls label SNR; applies to flow matching too). Add the two corner points to close the factorization.

### W4 (major). The \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) result reads as a rediscovery; the actual insight is understated, and the protocol's most important rule was not applied to the paper's own main comparison.

**What is wrong.** As stated ("trainability and robustness are functions of the executed exploration noise", L28, L50), the result is "more training noise gives more robustness", which the community knows from noise injection in imitation (DART), dynamics randomization, and action-noise studies. What is *new* here is (a) the coordinate: the executed noise, not the policy-side parameter, is what must be matched when comparing action spaces, and the filter's gain silently changes it -- this is why the seven-arm study, and likely many published comparisons, compared unmatched exploration; (b) the chunk-scale, colour-aware form \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\), whose mechanism (the policy learns to correct only displacements visible within one decision period) is a statement about *why* noise buys robustness, not just that it does; (c) the inverted-U location being interface-independent. The paper buries (a) as a "sixth rule" after the protocol list (L107) and gives (b) two sentences.

Also: the protocol says budgets must be matched on \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) (L107), yet the paper's own matched twin E5 is matched on \(\sigma\) (\(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) 0.030 vs E4's 0.069). The proposal lists a raw policy trained at \(\sigma_{\rm exec} = 0.069\) as pending (H7' iii); it never appeared. The paper tells readers to do a control it did not do for its central pair.

**Why a reviewer pushes.** "Known result" plus "you violated your own rule" is a two-line rejection paragraph.

**Fix.** Lead with the mediation claim, stated as such: "the robustness effect of action-space choice is fully mediated by executed chunk-scale exploration noise; after conditioning on it, structure has no residual effect except an observability penalty at two points." Promote \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) and its mechanism to the main statement and name the natural test (vary \(H\); the prediction is that the \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) collapse improves and the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) one does not). Either add the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\)-matched raw twin or state explicitly that the across-map correlation replaces the pairwise matched comparison and why.

### W5 (major). The "innovation" argument is imprecise, Proposition 1's "spectral exponent" is ill-defined, and the empirical "discovery" of whitening is an identity plus a prediction the paper does not make.

**What is wrong.** (i) L173: "the closed-loop derivative label ... is the innovation of the demonstration sequence, which is nearly white." The label is \((a_t-\lambda a_{t-1})/s\) when the filter tracks. That is the demonstration *pre-whitened by \(\lambda\)*; it is the innovation only if the demonstration command is AR(1) with coefficient exactly \(\lambda\). For an AR(1) demonstration with coefficient \(\phi\), the label's lag-1 autocorrelation is \((\phi-\lambda)(1-\lambda\phi)/(1+\lambda^2-2\lambda\phi)\): zero at \(\lambda=\phi\), negative above, positive below. With \(\phi\) between 0.77 and 0.80 (the raw policy's lag-1 autocorrelation, Table II) this predicts ac1(u) between -0.08 and -0.11 at \(\lambda = 0.9\)-0.95 (observed -0.11 to -0.14), near zero at \(\lambda = 0.8\), and positive at \(\lambda = 0.7\) (observed +0.40). The sign pattern in Table II is *predicted*, and the paper does not say so. (ii) Prop. 1 (L80): "low-frequency spectral exponent rising from 0 (\(\lambda = 0\)) to 2 (\(\lambda \to 1\))." For any \(\lambda < 1\) the AR(1) spectrum is flat below the corner frequency of about \((1-\lambda)/2\pi\) and rolls off at exponent 2 above it; the asymptotic low-frequency exponent is 0 for every \(\lambda < 1\). What the paper means is a band-limited fitted slope over the episode/chunk time-scales. A control-theory reviewer will flag the proposition as false as stated. (iii) The claim "this is not something RL learned" (L173) is asserted; it is checkable in the dataset (autocorrelation of the relabelled targets) and on the BC prior before RL, at zero rollout cost.

**Is it the real headline?** Yes -- but as the identity of W1, of which whitening is a corollary, not as "whitening." Presenting Table II as an empirical discovery undersells the logic (it had to happen) and oversells the surprise.

**Fix.** Replace "innovation" with "pre-whitened by \(\lambda\)" and add the one-line ac1 formula with the demonstration's measured autocorrelation; report ac1 of the dataset labels and of the BC prior next to the fine-tuned policy's; restate Prop. 1 in terms of corner frequency and roll-off, with the "\(\beta(\lambda)\)" mapping explicitly as a band-averaged fit (the pink-noise correspondence \(\lambda \approx 0.7\) to \(\beta \approx 1\) is then honest).

### W6 (major). Negative-result-about-our-own-proposal voice: currently a liability; it can be an asset.

**What is wrong.** L35 ("We started from exactly such a proposal ... Over ten days and roughly nine hundred evaluation rollouts ... This paper reports what survived"), L44 (a 13-line confessional of three mistakes), and protocol item 5 (L105: a per-episode buffer reset bug) put the paper in lab-notebook register. ICLR has room for negative results about *widely used* practices, not about an unpublished pilot. The protocol's "false conclusion it prevented" device is compelling only if the conclusions generalize beyond this project; as written, every instance is internal (one project), and item 5 is a software-engineering lesson, not methodology. L48 ("Most action-space papers report one point and no controls") and L33 ("These claims are usually supported by a single configuration compared against a single baseline") are uncited and read as a straw man.

**How to make it an asset.** Make the *target* the community practice, not the pilot: velocity/delta action spaces with previous-action conditioning are standard (robosuite OSC deltas, Diffusion Policy's velocity ablation, legged-locomotion policies with action history, driving's control-vs-trajectory outputs), and integrated/filtered exploration is standard (OU noise, gSDE, pink noise, chunking). Then "we show what this class of designs does and does not do" is a positive thesis with negative corollaries. Keep the self-correction story, compressed, as a boxed case study in the Discussion or appendix ("how matched-\(\sigma\) comparisons fooled us three times"), and state each control in general form in the main text. Drop protocol item 5 to the appendix; promote the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\)-matching rule to item 1. Cite the specific comparisons the protocol would have changed (W7 has candidates) or remove the "most papers" sentences.

### W7 (moderate). Related work misses the literature the reframed paper belongs to.

The bibliography (90 entries) is dominated by test-time-training papers from the abandoned framing; the 22 cited in the text leave out the core of the action-space, smoothness, exploration-noise, and imitation-label literatures that an ICLR reviewer will name. Concretely (all of these exist; verify details before citing):

- **Action-space choice in RL for control:** Peng and van de Panne (SCA 2017, torque vs PD vs velocity vs MTU action spaces for locomotion); Varin, Grossman and Kuindersma (IROS 2019, action spaces for manipulation); Martin-Martin et al. (IROS 2019, variable-impedance end-effector action space); Bogdanovic, Khadiv and Righetti (RA-L 2020); Aljalbout et al. (RA-L 2024, action space and sim-to-real). To my knowledge none matched *executed* noise across action spaces; that is exactly the gap the protocol names, and saying so with citations turns L33 from a straw man into a positioning statement.
- **Position vs velocity for diffusion/BC policies:** Chi et al.'s Diffusion Policy paper has a position-vs-velocity control ablation favouring position control; the present paper's result that a derivative prior with observable state is *better* (59.5 vs 38) is in apparent tension with it and should be discussed (the resolution is presumably conditioning + normalization, W3). Relative vs absolute action representations (ACT's absolute joint targets; UMI's relative trajectories) belong here too.
- **Smoothness regularization in RL:** CAPS (Mysore et al., ICRA 2021), Shen et al. (ICML 2020, robust and smooth policies). These smooth the *policy*, i.e., intent -- the thing the paper shows an observable filter cannot do.
- **Temporally correlated / filtered exploration:** OU noise (DDPG), gSDE (cited), pink noise (cited), autoregressive policies (Korenkevych et al., IJCAI 2019 -- AR(1) noise in action space, the closest prior to Prop. 1), Hollenstein et al. (TMLR 2022, action noise scale and colour vs performance), parameter-space noise (Plappert et al., ICLR 2018).
- **Chunking as a filter:** ACT's temporal ensembling and real-time chunking (Black et al., 2025) are literally low-pass operations across chunk boundaries; Q-chunking is cited but the connection ("a chunk is a zero-order-hold filter on both intent and noise") is not drawn.
- **Noise injection for robustness:** DART (Laskey et al., CoRL 2017), dynamics randomization (Peng et al., ICRA 2018), RARL (Pinto et al., ICML 2017). The paper's \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) result must be positioned against these or it will be read as their rediscovery.
- **Previous-action conditioning and its pathologies:** causal confusion (de Haan et al., NeurIPS 2019) and the copycat problem (Wen et al., NeurIPS 2020). See W8 -- this is not just related work, it is the missing hypothesis for the E4/F7 anomaly.
- **Residual / downstream corrections on frozen policies:** residual policy learning (Silver et al., 2018; Johannink et al., ICRA 2019), ResiP (Ankile et al., 2024), Policy Decorator (Yuan et al., 2024), DSRL (cited). The upstream/downstream placement result is a statement about this family.
- **Innovations view:** Kailath's innovations approach (1968) / Wold decomposition, if the paper keeps the word "innovation" at all.
- **Evaluation methodology in deep RL:** Henderson et al. (AAAI 2018), Engstrom et al. (ICLR 2020), Andrychowicz et al. (ICLR 2021), Agarwal et al. (NeurIPS 2021). The protocol should be positioned as an action-space-specific instance of this line, not only of "Rdumb" / "Parting with misconceptions".
- **Classical control:** the incremental (velocity-form) controller and delta-u parameterizations in MPC are the textbook ancestors of the derivative interface; noting that classical incremental control provides integral action *because the controller does not observe the integrator state* would sharpen the paper's finding that its policy provided none (L103).

### W8 (moderate). The E4/F7 anomaly is left "open" when it has a name and a test.

L204: "a policy observing \(c_{t-1}\) relies on the consistency between the command it observes and the motion it observes, which a gain change breaks." That is causal confusion of the copycat type: a policy conditioned on its own previous action can fit demonstrations by tracking the action history rather than the state, and actuator drift is exactly the intervention that breaks the shortcut. Naming it gives (a) a literature with remedies (history dropout, adversarial removal), (b) a prediction -- the penalty should scale with how much of the policy's state information is carried by \(c_{t-1}\) relative to the robot state, which the proposal already flirts with (|u| about 0.09 for E4, 0.40 for F7, both extremes) -- and (c) a cheap test (mask or noise \(c_{t-1}\) during BC and see whether the two outliers move to the trend). The paper does not need to resolve the anomaly, but "unexplained" is weaker than "consistent with a known failure mode, with the following discriminating test."

### W9 (moderate). Robotics-internal apparatus dominates Secs. VI-E and VI-F.

The gain estimator with dead-band trigger, the delay-aware estimator, the "misadaptation cost when the estimator is fooled", and the F6 configuration recommendation (L211-217, L228-230) are remnants of the abandoned TTT framing. For ICLR the only general result in VI-E is structural: *upstream corrections enjoy the filter's bound but are absorbed by an observable-state policy; downstream corrections work but are unprotected; therefore the protected adaptation site does not exist for observable-state interfaces.* That fits in one paragraph plus Fig. 3. The estimator design, the delay confound, and the sign of the misadaptation cost belong in an appendix. VI-F should be rewritten as the constructive corollary of the identity (choose \(\sigma\) so that \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) lands in the sweet spot, \(s\) for target SNR, \(\lambda\) for noise colour; do not expect smoothing of intent) rather than a per-axis scorecard.

### W10 (minor). Legibility.

- The twelve point codes (A, E5, B, F4, F5, F7, F1, F6, F2, F3, E4, E1) are historical artefacts of two studies; no reader can hold them. Rename by role: Raw-s.10, Raw-s.03, LP-s.10, LP-s.03, LP+obs-s.03, and so on, or by \((\lambda,s,\sigma,\text{obs})\).
- Table I has 17 columns and uses "Obs." for both "observable state" and "observation noise" (L124). Split into a design table and a results table.
- Fig. 1: four markers are stacked at (0.8, 0.2) with overlapping labels; the panel that carries the paper's main law (Fig. 2 right) should be Figure 1.
- Fig. 2 plots E3 and C, which are not in "the map" of twelve points (L48, L113); either add them to Table I or mark them as external.
- "Arm", "point", "configuration", "interface" are used interchangeably; pick one.
- "Matched-budget twin" (L44) is used before "budget" is defined as exploration noise.
- The "Propositions" (L79-91) are one-line linear-system facts; the subsection is honestly titled "Three linear-system facts" -- call them Facts or Lemmas, and reserve "Proposition" for the identity of W1.

### W11 (minor). Three sentences in tension with the paper's own thesis.

- L28/L204: "a raw policy trained with larger exploration noise is far more robust than any interface." If robustness is mediated by \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\), the thesis predicts a derivative interface at matched \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) would match the raw policy except for the observability penalty; the sentence reads as an interface-vs-raw ranking, which the paper itself argues is the wrong comparison. Say "at matched executed noise no interface beats the raw policy, and two observable-state points fall below the trend."
- L50 Contribution 3(d): "6.5x the training-seed variance." Ratio of two sample standard deviations from five seeds, driven by one seed at 77.5%. As a headline *finding* it is an anecdote; as a note that E4 sits at the edge of the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) sweet spot it is supportive. Demote it.
- L28: "the strongest raw baseline we could train." It is the raw policy at the DPPO default \(\sigma = 0.10\); only two raw \(\sigma\) values were trained. Say what it is. (The \(\sigma\) sweep on the raw arm is also the cleanest within-structure test of the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) law, since it varies noise with structure fixed.)

## 5. Proposal for the ICLR-shaped version

### Title

**The Policy Inverts the Filter: Derivative Action Spaces Reshape Exploration, Not Behaviour**

Alternative: *Action Filters Change the Noise, Not the Intent: An Identity and a Controlled Map for Derivative Action Spaces in RL Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Policies*

### Abstract (about 150 words)

> Velocity, increment, and "derivative" action spaces -- a policy output integrated or low-pass filtered before execution -- are adopted for smoother execution, easier RL fine-tuning, robustness, and safer adaptation. We show that all of them are one first-order filter, \(c_t=\lambda c_{t-1}+s u_t\), and that whenever the policy observes the filter state -- necessary for derivative labels to be identifiable -- the interface is an exact reparameterization of the position policy: it cannot alter the intended trajectory, only how perturbations reach the plant. We verify the corollaries on a twelve-configuration map for DPPO fine-tuning of diffusion policies: the policy output is the pre-whitened demonstration, deterministic execution is no smoother than demonstrations plus filtered model error, and upstream adaptive corrections are absorbed. Trainability and drift robustness are mediated by executed, chunk-scale exploration noise, not structure; the prior gain traces to previous-action conditioning and target scaling. A trainable derivative prior and a smoothing filter are mutually exclusive.

### Three contributions

1. **An identity and its consequences.** With the filter state observed, closed-loop relabelling is an exact change of coordinates (\(c_t=a_t\)); the interface is a position policy with previous-command conditioning and a rescaled target. Corollaries (each verified): (i) the policy output is the demonstration pre-whitened by \(\lambda\), with lag-1 autocorrelation predicted by \((\phi-\lambda)(1-\lambda\phi)/(1+\lambda^2-2\lambda\phi)\); (ii) the filter smooths only what is added in \(u\)-coordinates -- exploration noise, model error, adaptation noise -- never intent; (iii) upstream corrections are rejected as disturbances; (iv) impossibility: an unobserved filter smooths intent but has no identifiable derivative labels, so smoothing and a trainable derivative prior cannot coexist.
2. **Executed noise is the mediating variable.** Across twelve configurations, RL trainability (inverted U) and zero-shot drift robustness (monotone) are functions of the executed, chunk-scale exploration noise \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\); after conditioning on it, filter structure has no residual effect except a penalty at two observable-state points consistent with copycat-type causal confusion. Comparisons between action spaces must therefore match \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\), not the policy-side \(\sigma\); matched-\(\sigma\) comparisons produced +25 to +37 pp of spurious advantage in our own pilot.
3. **A decomposition of "what a derivative action space buys."** Its prior advantage is previous-command conditioning plus target normalization (\(s\) sets label SNR against the diffusion model's output-noise floor); its exploration advantage is coloured noise with tunable colour \(\lambda\); its costs are the observability penalty and the loss of a protected adaptation site. None of these requires integration. Four controls (matched-\(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) twin + strongest baseline, unobservable-filter null, direct mechanistic measurement, upstream/downstream injection) operationalize the decomposition for future action-space claims.

### Recommended section order

1. **Introduction** -- thesis first (the identity), then the three contributions, then one paragraph on why the community's velocity/delta/chunked designs are all instances. No project chronology.
2. **First-order action filters** -- definitions; **Proposition 1 (identity)** and its four corollaries; Facts 1-3 (spectral shaping with corner frequency, bounded propagation, execution-space trust region); the \(\sigma_{\rm exec}\) and \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\) definitions with the "displacement per decision period" mechanism.
3. **Related work** -- organized by the ingredients the identity exposes: action-space choice; previous-action conditioning and causal confusion; target normalization for generative policies; coloured exploration and chunking as filters; residual/downstream correction; evaluation methodology.
4. **Experimental design** -- the map as a factorial over \((\lambda,s,\sigma,\text{obs})\), including the two corner points \((0,1,\cdot,\text{obs})\) and \((0,s,\cdot,\text{obs})\) that close the factorization; the five axes in one table; the four controls in general form with one sentence each on what they rule out.
5. **Results, by channel** -- 5.1 Intent channel: prior (conditioning vs scaling), deterministic smoothness, whitening (dataset labels, then BC prior, then RL policy); 5.2 Perturbation channel: trainability and robustness vs \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\), adaptation placement; 5.3 The residual: E4/F7 and the copycat hypothesis with its test.
6. **What this changes for action-space design** -- five actionable rules (match \(\sigma_{\rm chunk}\); pick smoothing *or* a derivative prior; normalize derivative targets; expect drift fragility from command conditioning; do not expect a protected upstream adaptation site when the policy sees the filter). The F6 configuration as a worked example, one paragraph.
7. **Limitations** (as now) and **Appendix**: the case study "how matched-\(\sigma\) comparisons fooled us" (current L44, L98-107 material), estimator details, misadaptation suite, full tables.

### If the authors target CoRL/RA-L instead

The current draft is already an RA-L/ICRA-shaped paper, and the question "what does a derivative action space buy a diffusion policy" plus the F6 recommendation plus the protocol are valued there. What would differ: keep the protocol and the configuration recommendation as first-class contributions (they become Contributions 2 and 3), keep the five-axis map as the organizing figure, and keep VI-E's adaptation results at current length. What must not differ: W1 (state the identity -- it is also the best CoRL paper), W2 (the misreported statistic and the contradiction), W5 (innovation to pre-whitening), and W7 (action-space and exploration-noise related work). My recommendation is to do the ICLR reframing regardless, because the identity-first version is stronger at every venue; the only ICLR-specific cost is compressing VI-E/VI-F and the protocol into an appendix.

### Summary for the authors

The paper has one result an ICLR audience will remember -- an observed action filter is inverted by the policy, so it changes exploration and never intent, and smoothing and a trainable derivative prior are therefore mutually exclusive -- and one rule they will use: match executed, chunk-scale noise when comparing action spaces. Neither sentence appears in the draft. Write them first, derive the rest, fix the three claim/evidence mismatches in W2, add the two corner points that close the factorization, and move the project chronology and the estimator machinery to an appendix. The result would be a stronger paper at any of the three venues.
